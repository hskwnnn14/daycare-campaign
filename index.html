NYC · Medicaid social adult day care

Your parent’s day care was allowed 323 people.
What if it billed for 530?

That happened at one center in Flushing. This page is for the sons and daughters who drop their parents off, read the plan letters, and want the care to stay.

2 · Context

Who checks the center? The plans that get new members from it.

More than 400 centers serve about 45,000 seniors, and the program costs over $500 million a year. The state lets 35 private Medicaid plans check the same centers that bring them new members. Tap each part of the loop.

Nobody outside the loop counts who actually walks in the door.

Sources: NY Governor’s office, Oct. 2026; NYS Comptroller audit, Feb. 2026; author interview, Nov. 2026.

4 · Evidence II · No shared blacklist

$285 million was paid to centers after a plan had already cut them off.

When one plan drops a center, the center can keep billing the other plans. There is no shared list.

What this can’t showThat the money was stolen. The state health department says “questionable” payments aren’t proven fraud. Who paid whom by name is not public.
Source: NYS Office of the State Comptroller, audit, Feb. 2026 (plan payments 2019–2024). Zoomed view is drawn at its own scale.

5 · The turn

The state may shut the whole program down. That punishes your parent, not the people who cheated.

The governor’s office decides by Dec. 31, 2026. Pick a path to see who it hits.

Shut it down

  • About 45,000 seniors lose meals, rides and company
  • Honest centers close with the bad ones
  • People already charged were caught by investigators, not by the shutdown

Fix it

  • The state licenses every center itself
  • Real attendance checks: count who walks in
  • One shared list: cut off by one plan, cut off by all
